# Study-design reporting standards

Use this reference only when the manuscript's study design or target venue
requires a formal reporting guideline. A reporting guideline controls the
minimum information readers need; it is not a study-quality score and should
not turn the manuscript into a checklist narrative.

## Select by study design

| Study design | Starting guideline |
|---|---|
| Randomized trial | CONSORT; use the current statement and the applicable extension |
| Trial protocol | SPIRIT |
| Observational cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional study | STROBE and any applicable extension |
| Systematic review or meta-analysis | PRISMA; use PRISMA-P for a review protocol |
| Diagnostic-accuracy study | STARD |
| Prediction-model study | TRIPOD |
| Case report | CARE |
| Qualitative research | SRQR or COREQ, according to the method |
| Animal preclinical research | ARRIVE |
| Quality-improvement study | SQUIRE |
| Economic evaluation | CHEERS |
| Clinical-practice guideline | AGREE or RIGHT, according to the deliverable |

CONSORT's current general statement is CONSORT 2025. PRISMA's current general
systematic-review package remains PRISMA 2020. Do not infer that the general
statement is sufficient: search the official registry for design-, intervention-,
data-, and abstract-specific extensions before drafting a compliance claim.

## Apply the guideline without losing the paper

1. Identify the exact study design and target venue.
2. Retrieve the current official checklist and any applicable extension.
3. Build a private coverage map from each required item to the manuscript,
   figure, flow diagram, supplement, registration record, or submission form.
4. Add scientifically material missing information where readers need it.
5. Keep checklist administration, item numbers, and compliance notes outside
   the journal-facing narrative unless the venue explicitly asks for them.
6. Recheck the completed manuscript and submit the required checklist or flow
   diagram separately.

The checklist does not decide the paper's lead claim, comparison scope, or
result order. The academic-launch policy still controls those choices. Do not
invent missing enrollment, exclusions, outcomes, analyses, registration data,
ethics information, or protocol deviations. If required information is absent,
flag the exact missing item outside the manuscript for the author.
