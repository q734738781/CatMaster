# Section: Discussion (writing)

## Default structure

`central advance -> why the evidence supports it -> relation to the right prior work -> mechanism or interpretation -> scientific or practical consequence`

## Drafting rules

- Discussion **interprets**, it does not repeat Results figure by figure.
- Address a plausible rival explanation only when it could overturn the core interpretation; do not manufacture objections for completeness.
- Hedging strength must match evidence strength. Do not promote a "consistent with" finding to "demonstrates" wording.
- Do not add a generic limitations paragraph. If one condition materially changes the core interpretation, state that exact condition once and calibrate the claim around it without expanding it into a verdict on the whole method.

## Sentence syntax

Discussion sentences interpret:

- `may reflect`
- `suggests that`
- `could indicate`
- `is likely due to`
- `may facilitate`

## Common failure modes when drafting

- Re-summarizing Results instead of interpreting them.
- Leaving the paper's strongest advantage implicit.
- Expanding a local non-advantage into a general negative judgment.
- Ending on generic future work instead of the scientific consequence established here.

## Short rule to memorize

- Results = what we observed
- Discussion = why the strongest result matters and how we understand it
