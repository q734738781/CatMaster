# Presentations and visual acceptance

Let the scientific argument guide the pages. Put the decisive results,
comparisons and necessary interpretation on the slides themselves. Notes can
carry spoken explanations and secondary detail; they cannot be the only home
of the main finding. Concise labels and genuinely parallel bullets are useful.

Choose layouts after identifying what readers must compare or understand.
Structures, curves and annotated experimental images often communicate more
than isolated numbers. Use native editable text, tables and page objects when
editability is requested; embed data figures or complex illustrations as assets.
Distinguish explanatory schematics from observed structures and measurements.

Inspect actual rendered pages for content, clipping, overlap and legibility.
Technical editability and a successful render do not establish scientific
communication. Check captions, units, axes, conditions and attribution needed
to interpret the evidence; explain what the reader should notice.

Where delegation is available, divide context-heavy inspection into coherent
sections with enough neighboring context to judge transitions. Each check
returns located findings and relevant source paths. Whole-document integration
still needs overall coverage and argument judgment, with selective page reading
to resolve gaps. It need not import every source image and previous render into
one conversation. An isolated check does not automatically accept the document.

Repair concrete obstacles at their actual scope: an unexplained comparison,
illegible labels or a missing causal connection. Inspect the changed pages and
their connections, including requested speaker notes. Preserve approved content
in format-only work. Use existing evidence and stop when the requested content
and visual communication work; no fixed page count, image quota, review cycle
or separate quality report is required.
