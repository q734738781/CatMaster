# Scientific communication

## Write for the requested reader

Use the user's question, intended audience and requested product to choose what
needs explaining. For reports and talks without a stated audience, assume a
research advisor or experimental collaborator unfamiliar with this project's
notation and history. An internal technical record can use concise shorthand;
a manuscript follows its scientific contribution and venue. Preserve approved
structure and voice unless the requested revision changes them.

A literature synthesis explains developments, route differences and what they
mean. A progress report explains findings and why they changed the next question.
A paper argues its supported contribution. None requires a universal chapter
order, a fixed number of claims or a prescribed opening sentence.

## Develop the scientific explanation

Write as a knowledgeable colleague helping an attentive reader understand a real
question. Decide what the reader should learn and why it matters. Select the
observations that explain it, develop their relationships and make the judgment
supported by the evidence clear. Each passage should advance understanding;
its form follows the question. Spend words where they add explanatory value:
a decisive comparison can deserve sustained discussion while familiar background
can remain implicit. Depth comes from developing the important points.

Trust the reader's shared knowledge and ability to infer ordinary implications.
A supported finding can stand on its own. Avoid competing explanations and
qualifications.

Make the scientific object concrete: the material, composition, reaction,
structure or system behind the numbers. Introduce the question and explain why
the comparison addresses it. Connect observations to interpretation and practical
or scientific consequences; distinguish an observed result from a proposed
mechanism or your synthesis. Methods deserve detail when they answer the question.

Use decisive quantitative anchors with the conditions, units and uncertainty
needed to interpret them. Let the actual comparison determine caption content.
When a table or figure contains the values, explain its important contrast and
implication in the text. Dates, source-access details and verification history
belong in the narrative only when they affect the requested interpretation.

Preserve negative findings and uncertainty that change the interpretation.
Reuse established checks; read a relevant source or visual when needed to explain
it, without restarting its validation. A missing visual can warrant preparing a
faithful display from existing evidence, not new research.

## Keep prose clear

Use concrete subjects, precise verbs and connected paragraphs. Choose lists and
tables for information readers need to compare or follow. Maintain transitions
and explain scientific meaning without promotional claims, generic filler or
repetitive disclaimers. Preserve numbers, units, equations, citations, uncertainty
and claim strength during language revision.

Write natural Chinese with the subjects, verbs and relationships needed to
understand the point. Use established technical terms; do not invent compressed
noun strings or join status fragments with slashes to fit a heading, table cell
or slide. State what is known about the material, what remains uncertain and why
a recommendation follows. Working notes' shorthand is not ready-to-publish prose.

Use classifications only when they help the reader compare or decide. Choose
dimensions for the reader's actual question, not from a preset taxonomy or a
previous report. Keep distinct judgments separate, use consistent terms and
explain unfamiliar categories once. Introduce necessary IDs and abbreviations with the
object they identify and their meaning; retain exact reference identifiers where
useful. Incidental tracking codes belong in source records, not the explanation.
A familiar scientific term or a user-defined sample name needs no invented synonym.

Treat evidence notes and suggested wording as working material. Choose what the
reader needs even when a supplied note emphasizes caution. A production instruction
guides the work; compliance belongs in the prose only when the process itself is
relevant to the requested explanation. Integration includes this editorial choice,
along with a coherent argument across sections.

When wording is formulaic or the user asks for prose polishing, consult the
[phrasing reference](references/phrasing.md) for relevant examples. It is an
editorial aid, not a banned-word detector or a required pass through every row.
Match the language and voice of the document; do not force English patterns onto
Chinese prose. Deliver the requested text or artifact, without intermediate
drafts or an editing checklist unless requested.

## Use compact, recoverable handoffs

Summarize the question, useful findings, their meaning, source paths and the
user's explicit requirements. Leave unstated writing choices to the author's
judgment and disciplinary conventions. Avoid adding writing prohibitions or
lists of hypothetical misunderstandings to prevent. Keep conditions with the
evidence they qualify so the author can judge their relevance; they are not a
checklist of sentences to include. Preserve the target and scope of user feedback
without expanding it into new restrictions. A compact brief leaves fuller
evidence reachable and the document's organization open.

Split long work into coherent sections or question groups with enough shared
purpose and neighboring context to join up. Authors may reorganize the argument
within the requested scope. Keep sources reachable for selective reading; avoid
copying complete research histories into every writing task.

## Judge the actual deliverable

Read the document against the question and audience. Check the overall argument,
coverage, transitions and relevance to the reader as well as local facts. When
reading headings, tables and conclusions, check that their meaning is clear
without internal notes; expand opaque shorthand into the actual finding or
recommendation, rather than merely defining a new vocabulary of labels. When
qualifications or production commentary crowd out findings and implications,
correct the explanation at that scope. If a chapter answers the wrong question,
correct the chapter's purpose and explanation. Local numerical fixes
alone do not repair that problem. Reuse completed checks and inspect affected
content after revision; stop when the requested communication works.

For presentations or context-heavy visual inspection, read
[presentations and visual acceptance](references/presentations.md).
