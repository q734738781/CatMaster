# Research direction recovery

## Recover the decision the user needs

Read the original objective and steering alongside actual Methods and Results.
Keep user constraints separate from choices introduced in a task brief. A request
to reduce costly calculations does not by itself prohibit inexpensive learning,
source discovery or reuse of existing data. A convenient model, available package,
or completed comparison is not the definition of the research goal.

Locate the unresolved decision: which explanation remains plausible, what model
can guide the intended choice, or which candidate deserves the next experiment.
Preserve useful partial results. Ask whether the evidence answers that decision
or only a smaller implementation question.

## Let relevant literature change the method

Search from the scientific task, changing chemical or physical factors, and data
regime before committing to a familiar algorithm or checkpoint. Compare nearby
applications and materially different representations or learning strategies.
Read enough of decisive methods to distinguish target labels, source data,
adaptation, interactions represented, and validation from a paper's headline.
Availability is one feasibility consideration, not the sole relevance criterion.

A failed frozen readout tests that representation and adaptation procedure; it
does not reject an entire model family. An encoding that misses a task-critical
distinction motivates revisiting the representation. Keep the smallest useful
set of alternatives with concrete reasons for attempting or deferring each;
neither exhaustive model sweeps nor a fixed number of papers is the objective.

## Connect findings to action

When modeling serves candidate selection, use its actual outputs to compare the
candidate space and explain the resulting choice. Separate predicted improvement
from discriminating information and from experimental confirmation. Branches may
work concurrently, but their results should change the shared decision where
relevant. A chemistry proposal and a model benchmark do not automatically form a
model-guided recommendation merely because both are complete.

Before waiting for a user-supplied library or other input, determine whether the
authorized public-source or workspace capabilities can obtain what is needed.
Keep genuinely user-controlled inputs and real resource boundaries explicit.
Do not turn an unexamined acquisition option into an external dependency.

## Make a challenge useful and bounded

Identify the consequential premise, the evidence that challenges or supports it,
and the next feasible observation or analysis that would change the decision.
Explain what each plausible outcome means. Reuse completed comparisons and source
work; a new name or restated concern does not justify repeating them.

Novelty, a positive model result and disagreement are not required outcomes.
Conclude that stopping is justified when the actual objective is met or no useful
authorized action remains. Preserve the unresolved premise and what new evidence
would reopen it. Do not expand a bounded report or recommendation into unauthorized
calculations or experimental execution.
