# Submission and Editorial Readiness

Use this reference during final writing and review, especially for journal-facing drafts.

This checklist is distilled from common high-standard journal expectations, including Nature-style initial submission discipline:

- the manuscript should present one clear advance
- claims should be significant without being inflated
- data, methods, and figure provenance should be inspectable
- author-facing declarations should be complete and explicit

## Editorial readiness checks

- The title is specific and not slogan-like.
- The abstract states what was done and what was found, not just what is hoped.
- The introduction defines the gap early.
- The discussion does not outrun the evidence.
- Methods are sufficient for review and reproduction.
- Figure legends are self-contained.
- Source data, scripts, or artifact references are available where needed.

## Integrity checks

- Authorship assumptions are not invented in the manuscript body.
- Competing interests, funding, and acknowledgments are kept factual.
- Data or code availability claims match the actual repository or artifact paths.
- Supplementary claims are not required to rescue weak main-text logic.

## Revision readiness

Before marking a draft as complete, confirm:

1. every major claim has an evidence anchor
2. every figure and table is cited in order
3. every citation supports the sentence it is attached to
4. limitations are explicit
5. the manuscript can survive a hostile but fair review without hidden assumptions

## Cover-letter mindset for the manuscript itself

Even before a cover letter exists, the manuscript should make it obvious:

- what the main contribution is
- why the target audience should care
- what evidence makes the claim credible
- what the main boundary conditions are
